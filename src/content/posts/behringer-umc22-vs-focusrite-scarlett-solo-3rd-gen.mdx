## Quick verdict

The **Behringer UMC22** and the **Focusrite Scarlett Solo 3rd Gen** are both small, USB-powered interfaces with one mic input and one instrument input. The **Solo** has the stronger specs (24-bit/192kHz), a big software bundle, and owners rate its sound clean and quiet. Its main risk is problems that show up after weeks or months. The **UMC22**'s top praise is its low price. Its main risks are Windows drivers and hiss.

- **Pick the Scarlett Solo** if sound quality and low noise matter most to you, or you want the software bundle.
- **Pick the UMC22** if price comes first, especially if you're on Mac or Linux. The few Mac and Linux owners in our sample report easy setups.

## Which one sounds better?

Owners favor the **Scarlett Solo**. Several describe its recordings as clean, and two who had noise problems with other interfaces say it's very quiet. On the **UMC22**, more than a dozen owners report hiss, static or a high noise floor, and about half a dozen found the sound muddy or muffled. Three UMC22 owners who moved to a Focusrite Scarlett or a PreSonus Studio 24c were happier with the sound.

It's not one-sided. Two UMC22 owners say a Shure SM57 sounded very good through it, and several owners single out its Midas preamp. One UMC22 owner calls it ultra quiet.

## Which one is easier to set up?

The **Scarlett Solo** on Windows, based on owner reports. The UMC22's biggest complaint is Windows drivers. Behringer's specs say Windows drivers are available to download from its site, but owners say the site offers ASIO4ALL, a generic driver, and many struggled to get it working on Windows 10 or 11, or returned it. One owner says ASIO4ALL locks the interface to one app at a time.

The Solo isn't trouble-free. Several owners found its bundled software slow to download or hard to install, and several report crackle or static on Windows, especially on Discord and during streams.

On other systems, one UMC22 owner on a Mac says it was recognized right away, and one on Debian Linux says it was plug-and-play. One Solo owner uses it on Linux as an output device.

## Which one lasts longer?

This is the Solo's weak spot. More than a dozen Solo owners in our sample say it worked well at first, then developed dropouts, crackling, static or lock-ups after days, weeks or months. Four Solo owners also had USB port or cable problems.

UMC22 owners are mixed. Two had connection problems after a few months, such as inputs or outputs cutting out, while others have used theirs for many years, one for 12.

## Which one has better mic gain?

Neither is clearly ahead. Four Solo owners found its mic signal too quiet, though others were happy with it. On the UMC22, one owner coming from an older Scarlett prefers its gain controls, and one owner with a Sennheiser e945 says it drove the mic well enough, though an inline booster helped. One UMC22 guitarist found its two inputs had very different gain levels.

## Who should buy which?

**Get the Scarlett Solo 3rd Gen if you:**
- Care most about clean, quiet recordings, which owners praise on the Solo
- Use Windows and don't want to hunt for drivers
- Want 24-bit/192kHz and the Ableton Live Lite and Pro Tools Intro+ bundle
- Like a gain halo that shows your level right on the knob
