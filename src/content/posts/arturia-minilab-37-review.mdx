## Quick verdict

The **Arturia MiniLab 37** is a 37-key MiniLab. It has 8 RGB pads with two banks (16 pad slots), 8 knobs, 4 faders, touch strips for pitch and mod, a small OLED screen, and an arpeggiator, Chord mode and Hold. Most MiniLab 37 owners we read are very happy: they praise the keys, the easy setup and the software bundle. The pads are the main complaint, from owners of both the 37 and the 25-key MiniLab 3.

## Should you buy the MiniLab 37?

**Yes,** if you want great-feeling keys, faders and knobs, and a software bundle to start making music with, and 25 keys feel too few. **No,** if finger drumming on the pads matters to you. Two MiniLab 37 owners found the pads hard to play, and one suggests a different controller if pads are your priority.

## What MiniLab 37 owners say

We read 9 reviews of the MiniLab 37: seven 5-star, one 4-star and one 2-star.

**What they like**
- **The keys.** One owner who has bought plenty of keyboards under $200 was impressed by them. Another calls the key feel the best around and unbeatable for the price, though they find the velocity response not the most gradual.
- **Easy setup.** Owners call it easy to set up and easy to start using with a computer. One found it easier to configure and map in their DAW than their old Akai.
- **DAW use.** One owner says it works perfectly with Ableton Live 11 and with other DAWs on Mac and Windows. Another uses it with Logic.
- **Software and sounds.** Owners praise the software bundle and the sounds. One likes Arturia's app for keeping it up to date.
- **Faders.** One owner likes the faders' feel and finds that, with the DAW and plugins, they mean less reaching for the mouse.
- **Build and finish.** Owners mention its quality and good finish. Even the owner who rated it 2 stars found everything apart from the pads high quality.
- **USB-C and connectivity.** One owner is glad it uses USB-C, and another praises its connectivity.

**What they don't like**
- **The pads.** One owner says you have to hit them very hard to register, a medium tap barely registers, and changing the velocity curve doesn't solve it. Another says light presses don't sound while a slightly harder hit jumps to full velocity, so they don't suit complex grooves. One says the Akai MPK Mini mk4 has better pads, and the other wishes the pads felt more like the Akai MPK Mini 4's.
- **The knobs, for one owner.** They don't love Arturia's knobs. Another owner says the knobs work well.
- **Alternatives.** The owner who rated it 2 stars suggests the Arturia KeyStep 37 mk2 instead, which they say has more features but no faders.

## What's in the box and the software

- MiniLab 37 and a USB-C to USB-A cable. Two MiniLab 3 owners say their unit arrived without the cable.
- **Included software:** Analog Lab Intro, Ableton Live Lite, a Native Instruments Komplete 15 Select bundle of your choice (Beats, Band or Electronic), 2 months of Loopcloud with 2 sample packs, a Melodics trial and the MIDI Control Center app.
- **You need an Arturia account.** MiniLab 3 owners say you register the unit on Arturia's website to unlock the software. Arturia's ASC app installs and updates its instruments.

*Arturia's software bundle may change. Check the current list before you buy.*

## What MiniLab 3 owners say

These are reviews of the 25-key MiniLab 3, which is sold on the same Amazon listing. Its pad layout differs from the 37's, so take its pad and size feedback with that in mind.

**What they like**
- **The keys.** This is the most consistent praise. One owner who compared four mini controllers side by side found the MiniLab's keys the best of them. Another calls them top tier among mini controllers. One owner liked it so much they wish they'd bought the 37.
- **Knobs and faders.** Owners describe them as solid and good quality. One owner likes that they have some weight, so you don't jump a value by accident.
- **Portability.** Owners take the 25-key model to the park, on trips and to live sets. The 37 is wider, at 503 mm.
- **Value and sounds.** Several owners call it good value, and several praise Analog Lab.
- **Works with many DAWs.** Owners report using it with Ableton Live, Logic Pro, FL Studio, Reaper, Cubase and Mixcraft.

**Common complaints**
- **Pads.** Several owners found the pads stiff and hard to trigger. Others had pads that wobble, double-trigger or jump to full velocity too easily. One owner had two pads stop working.
- **Registration and software install.** One owner got codes that were shown as invalid, two found the unit was already registered, and one owner's registration kept failing. Some had Analog Lab crash or show a blank screen, and some say Arturia support was slow to reply or didn't reply.
- **DAW setup.** Several owners found setup confusing and needed outside tutorials. One had functions that didn't work in FL Studio. One owner felt it gives little control over Ableton's Session view.
- **Settings need a computer.** One owner was frustrated that you can't change the MIDI channel or pad notes on the device itself.
- **Build.** One owner says the keyboard flexes in the middle. Another found it cheap-feeling, and a third says the build uses more plastic than the older MiniLab mk2.
