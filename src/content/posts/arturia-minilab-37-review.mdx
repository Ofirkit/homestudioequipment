## Quick verdict

The **Arturia MiniLab 37** is the MiniLab 3 with 37 keys instead of 25. It has 16 RGB pads in two banks, 8 knobs, 4 faders, touch strips for pitch and mod, a small screen, and an arpeggiator, Chord mode and Hold. Owners love the key feel, the knobs and faders, and how easy it is to carry. The most common complaints are the pads and getting the software registered and set up.

## Should you buy the MiniLab 37?

**Yes,** if you want great-feeling keys and hands-on knobs in a controller that still fits on a small desk or in a bag, and the MiniLab 3's 25 keys feel too few. **No,** if finger drumming on the pads matters to you, or if you want a controller that works fully without any software setup.

## What owners of the 37-key model say

Only three of the reviews we read were of the MiniLab 37, so most of this review draws on the 25-key MiniLab 3.

- **One owner is very happy.** They've owned plenty of keyboards under $200 and were impressed by the keys and controls. They also like the USB-C port and found it easy to set up with Logic.
- **One owner returned it over the pads.** They had to hit the pads very hard to register a note, and changing the velocity curve didn't fix it. They found the rest of the controller high quality.
- **One owner hadn't used it yet** but rates Arturia highly.

## What's in the box and the software

- MiniLab 37 and a USB-C to USB-A cable. Two MiniLab 3 owners say their unit arrived without the cable.
- **Included software:** Analog Lab Intro, Ableton Live Lite, a Native Instruments Komplete 15 Select bundle of your choice (Beats, Band or Electronic), 2 months of Loopcloud with 2 sample packs, a Melodics trial and the MIDI Control Center app.
- **You need an Arturia account.** Owners say you register the unit on Arturia's website to unlock the software. Arturia's ASC app installs and updates its instruments.

*Arturia's software bundle may change. Check the current list before you buy.*

## What owners like

- **The keys.** This is the most consistent praise. Owners call the keys responsive and easy to play. One owner who compared four mini controllers side by side found the MiniLab's keys the best of them, the closest to real keys. Another calls them top tier among mini controllers.
- **Knobs and faders.** Owners describe the knobs and faders as solid and good quality. One owner likes that the faders have some weight, so you don't jump a value by accident.
- **Portability.** Owners take it to the park, on trips and to live sets. One owner who later bought a bigger Arturia keyboard still values the MiniLab for its size.
- **Value and sounds.** Many owners call it good value. Several praise Analog Lab, and one owner who started with it went on to buy more Arturia software.
- **Works with many DAWs.** Owners report using it with Ableton Live, Logic Pro, FL Studio, Reaper, Cubase and Mixcraft. One owner says it worked straight out of the box with Logic, and another says it worked smoothly with Ableton Live Lite once the software was installed.
- **Arpeggiator and touch strips.** One owner likes holding a chord while the arpeggiator runs. Owners who mention the touch strips found them responsive.
- **Looks.** Some owners like the clean design and wood-look side panels. Others find the panels tacky.

## Common complaints

**Pads (the most common hardware complaint)**
- Several owners found the pads stiff and hard to trigger. One 37-key owner says a medium tap barely registers.
- Others had the opposite problem: pads that wobble, double-trigger or jump to full velocity too easily.
- One owner had two pads stop working and returned the unit.

**Registration and software install**
- Several owners had trouble registering the unit. Some got invalid codes, some found the unit was already registered, and one owner's registration kept failing.
- Some owners had Analog Lab crash or show a blank screen. One owner couldn't install Ableton Live Lite on an older PC.
- A few owners found the install heavy, with many separate apps and files.
- Some owners say Arturia support was slow to reply or didn't reply.

**DAW setup**
- Several owners found setup confusing and needed outside tutorials. One owner says it's not as ready to use out of the box as Arturia suggests.
- One owner had functions that didn't work in FL Studio, and another had trouble in GarageBand on iPad.
- One owner couldn't get the knobs working in Logic Pro.

**Settings need a computer**
- One owner who uses hardware gear was frustrated that you can't change the MIDI channel or pad notes on the device itself. They also wanted a MIDI input.
- One owner found the minimal labeling clean but hard to remember. The same owner notes the transport controls share buttons with the pads.

**Build and units**
- One owner says the keyboard flexes in the middle. Another found it cheap-feeling.
- One owner got a unit with a dead key.
- One owner who moved from the MiniLab mk2 found the newer knobs stiffer and harder to turn quickly.
