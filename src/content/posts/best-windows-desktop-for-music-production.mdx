## Quick verdict

A beginner's Windows desktop for music production needs a current-generation 6- to 8-core CPU, 16 GB of RAM (32 GB if you'll use sample libraries), an NVMe SSD and a quiet case. The graphics card barely matters. Every DAW maker's own requirements point the same way, and the three builds and three prebuilt desktops below are chosen to meet them.

**If you want one answer:** the sweet-spot build, a Ryzen 7 9700X or Core i7-14700K with 32 GB of DDR5 and a 1 TB NVMe drive. If you plan to run Pro Tools, take the Intel version: Avid does not approve AMD processors.

## What actually matters in a music production PC?

### CPU: speed and cores, from a recent generation

Ableton's advice is "the fastest processor you can afford, with as many cores as possible," and it says single-core speed is just as important as core count because signal paths merge at summing points where parallel processing has to wait. On Intel's hybrid chips, Ableton processes audio only on the Performance cores, so Efficiency cores don't add audio capacity.

Steinberg says a newer processor generation is usually preferable and that going beyond 32 logical cores gives no significant benefit. Native Instruments suggests at least four cores with hyperthreading, and eight for larger sessions. Sweetwater's floor is a 64-bit Core i5 or Ryzen 5 3600.

For a beginner that means a current 6- to 8-core desktop chip: Ryzen 5/7 or Core i5/i7. Pro Tools users: Intel only.

### RAM: 16 GB is the floor, 32 GB is comfortable

Cubase 16 and Pro Tools both list 16 GB as the minimum. Ableton recommends 16 GB and says 32 GB or more for large projects with extensive sample libraries; when RAM runs out, Live swaps to disk, which causes dropouts and clicks. Native Instruments says to use matched modules (two of the same size) rather than mixing sizes.

### Storage: SSD only, ideally NVMe

Ableton wants an SSD as the system drive, says SSDs reduce dropouts when streaming samples, and asks for at least 10% free space. Steinberg calls SSDs the superior choice and the first choice for sample streaming. Puget Systems uses SSDs only, prefers M.2 NVMe, and recommends two drives: one for Windows and applications, one for projects and samples. Focusrite says to keep more than 20% of the boot drive free.

A 1 TB NVMe drive covers Windows, your DAW and its bundled content. A second drive can come later.

### Graphics: nearly irrelevant

Native Instruments calls the GPU the least relevant component for real-time audio. Ableton lists a dedicated GPU as optional, mainly for video work. Puget Systems ranks it among the least important parts but notes a card is still useful for multiple monitors. Integrated graphics on a current CPU is enough.

### Audio: use an external ASIO interface

Ableton calls ASIO the best performing audio driver type on Windows and says external interfaces with optimized drivers allow lower buffer sizes and lower latency than built-in audio. Steinberg requires ASIO 2.0 compatible drivers for very low latency. Puget Systems recommends an external interface over an internal sound card, partly to keep audio away from the noisy inside of the PC. If you don't have one yet, see our [Focusrite Scarlett Solo 3rd Gen review](/focusrite-scarlett-solo-3rd-gen-review/) or our [Behringer UMC22 vs Scarlett Solo comparison](/behringer-umc22-vs-focusrite-scarlett-solo-3rd-gen/).

### USB ports

Focusrite says USB hubs are known to cause issues with audio devices and to connect the interface directly to the computer. Count the rear USB-A ports: an interface, a MIDI keyboard, a mouse, a keyboard and a licence dongle add up fast. We aimed for six or more.

### Noise

Puget Systems says low noise is extremely important, especially when recording music, and that hard drives are often the loudest part of a computer. Native Instruments pairs an 8-core CPU with an efficient heatsink and a case that cools well. Steinberg says a CPU needs stable clocks for low-latency work, which means the cooler has to cope.

### Operating system: Windows 11, 64-bit

Microsoft ended Windows 10 support on October 14, 2025. Steinberg recommends Windows 11 64-bit, and the current Pro Tools release is for Windows 11 only. Sweetwater says Windows Home is sufficient for recording.

## What should you skip?

- **A big graphics card.** Every source ranks it last. Money moved from the GPU to RAM or a second SSD buys more for audio.
- **A hard drive as the main drive.** Slower loads, more dropouts when streaming samples, and often the loudest part of the PC.
- **USB hubs for the audio interface.** Focusrite says they are known to cause issues; plug the interface straight into the PC.
- **Windows 10 machines.** Out of Microsoft support since October 14, 2025; the current Pro Tools release is Windows 11 only.
- **CPUs without AVX2.** Live 12 will not launch.
- **AMD for Pro Tools.** Avid says AMD processors are not approved for Pro Tools and Pro Tools Ultimate.
- **Older budget chips like the Ryzen 5 5500.** No integrated graphics, PCIe 3.0 only and DDR4 only; the Core i5-12400 costs a similar amount and avoids all three.
