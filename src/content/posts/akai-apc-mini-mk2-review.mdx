## Quick verdict

The **Akai APC Mini MK2** is a compact pad controller with 64 RGB pads and 9 faders, built for launching clips and mixing in Ableton Live. Owners like its small size, how quickly it works with Ableton, and how far it can be pushed beyond Ableton: DJ software, other DAWs and even stage lighting. The biggest complaints are pads that look dim, a confusing setup and software process, and units that stopped working or lost their lights after weeks or months.

## Should you buy the APC Mini MK2?

**Yes,** if you use Ableton Live 11 or newer and want a small controller for launching clips and controlling levels, or if you're happy to map it yourself for DJ software or lighting. **No,** if you want something that works with no setup, need bright pads in a well-lit room, or want velocity-sensitive pads.

## What's in the box and the software

- APC Mini MK2, USB cable, user guide, software download cards, and a safety and warranty manual
- Ableton Live Lite (download)
- One owner mentions 6 months of free music samples after registering.

## What owners like

- **Ableton integration.** One owner using Ableton Live Suite 12 says Live recognized it out of the box and calls it especially good for clip launching and fader adjustments. Another says it worked the first time they plugged it in, with no syncing needed.
- **Small and portable.** Owners repeatedly mention how light and compact it is. One DJ keeps it near the desk, and a lighting tech says it fits in a gig bag.
- **A DJ controller, too.** One DJ mapped it to Traktor and Virtual DJ in three days, controlling stems, pitch, key, hot cues, loops and effects from one page. They use it alongside their main deck at gigs and call it a super good value at $100.
- **Lighting control.** Four owners use it to run stage lighting with SoundSwitch or Chauvet ShowXpress. One says the RGB pads let them set a fixture's actual color, and another says the lit pads help with staying organized in a dark booth.
- **Programmable for other software.** One owner uses it in Reaper with custom "modes" and programmed the pad colors too, though it took some work. Another uses Note mode as a cheap grid-layout chromatic keyboard.
- **Faders and pads, for some.** One owner describes the faders as consistent and smooth, though not Innofaders, and another says they're great for controlling lighting dimmers. Two owners call the pads responsive, and one Italian owner found the buttons soft and sensitive.
- **Improvements over the original.** One owner who used the first APC Mini says the MK2 feels heavier and sturdier, with a firmer USB connection, and that RGB pads made a huge difference.
- **Value against bigger controllers.** One owner recommends it to anyone who doesn't want to pay for an APC40, and another says it's about a quarter of the price of a Launchpad X.

## Common complaints

**Pad brightness and colors (the most common hardware complaint)**
- Six owners found the pads dim or the colors dull. Two say they're hard to see in daylight or very bright rooms, and one of them says it's not a problem in dark rooms. One owner says orange looks pink and pink looks brown.
- Four owners couldn't get the pads to light up at all, or found the lighting stopped working. One owner says the RGB lights work with Ableton Live 11.2.7 and above.

**Setup, software and documentation**
- More than a dozen owners found setup confusing. Complaints include unclear instructions, a manual that doesn't explain enough, a learning curve, and having to install several pieces of software, including the inMusic download manager and an iLok license.
- One owner explains that when Ableton isn't running, it shows up as two MIDI devices, and the second one is the Note mode output. They sometimes have to reset MIDI settings or open and close Ableton to get it working.
- One owner using it with Voicemeeter says there's very little documentation on how to send it the right MIDI commands. Another, who tested it with their own MIDI tester, says Akai support couldn't help.
- One owner found it hard to use alongside other controllers in Ableton. Another, using it with an Ableton Push 2 in Live 12, had sound randomly stop and volume jumps when switching songs.
- One owner almost returned it because it didn't show up in the DAW. They say new Windows MIDI updates don't currently work with some Akai products, and suggest searching online for a solution.

**Ableton Live Lite and software access**
- Several owners had trouble getting the included software. One owner's Ableton code had expired, another still didn't have Live Lite after 3 days, and another couldn't download the software on a Samsung laptop.
- One owner says only the Ableton Live Lite key worked, not the keys for Akai's software. Another found the temporary DAW download stopped working after a day.
- Two owners say they needed a paid account or license to use it. The Amazon listing doesn't mention a subscription.

**Reliability**
- Three owners say it worked at first and then failed: after a week, after 2 months, or with MIDI mappings that "went crazy" after a few months. That last owner says there's no factory reset.
- Two owners had LEDs fail. One has had a blue LED stop working on three different units, and another has two units that both have several failed LEDs.
- One owner says it randomly stops working until you unplug it and plug it back in, which makes it not so great for live performance.
- One owner's unit turned off after 5 minutes and didn't turn on again. Others received units that wouldn't turn on, arrived damaged, or had a bad USB port.

**Build, pads and faders**
- One owner says it feels like a toy, the build isn't great and the faders are just OK, and that it should be cheaper. Another says it's strong but not super rugged.
- One owner found the pads weak, and another found them a bit more sensitive than they like.
- One owner says Note mode isn't velocity sensitive.
- One owner wished it had USB-C instead of USB-B.

**Packaging**
- Two owners say it arrived with no outer packaging or with a large hole in the box, and another's unit arrived damaged.
