## Quick verdict

The **Akai APC Mini MK2** is a compact pad controller with 64 RGB pads and 9 faders, built for launching clips and mixing in Ableton Live. Owners like its small size, how quickly it works with Ableton, and how far it can be pushed beyond Ableton: DJ software, other DAWs and even stage lighting. The biggest complaints are pads that look dim, a confusing setup and software process, and units that stopped working or lost their lights after weeks or months.

## Should you buy the APC Mini MK2?

**Yes,** if you use Ableton Live 11 or newer and want a small, inexpensive way to launch clips and control mixer levels, or if you're happy to map it yourself for DJ software or lighting. **No,** if you want something that works with no setup, need bright pads for daylight or stage use, or want velocity-sensitive pads for finger drumming.

## What's in the box and the software

- APC Mini MK2, USB cable, user guide, software download cards, and a safety and warranty manual
- Ableton Live Lite (download)
- One owner mentions 6 months of free music samples after registering.

*Bundle details are from the Amazon listing, require registration and download, and may change.*

## What owners like

- **Ableton integration.** One owner using Ableton Live Suite 12 says Live recognized it out of the box. Another says it worked the first time they plugged it in, with no syncing needed. Owners call it especially good for clip launching and fader adjustments.
- **Small and portable.** Owners repeatedly mention how light and compact it is. One DJ keeps it near their desk and moves it out of the way after work, and a lighting tech says it fits in their gig bag.
- **A DJ controller, too.** One DJ mapped it to Traktor and Virtual DJ in three days, controlling stems, pitch, key, hot cues, loops and effects from one page. They use it alongside their main deck at gigs and call it a strong value at around $100.
- **Lighting control.** Four owners use it to run stage lighting with SoundSwitch or Chauvet ShowXpress. One says the RGB pads let them set a fixture's actual color, and another says the lit pads help them stay organized in a dark booth.
- **Programmable for other software.** One owner uses it in Reaper with custom "modes" and programmed the pad colors themselves, though it took some work. Another uses Note mode as a cheap grid-layout chromatic keyboard.
- **Faders and pads, for many.** Owners describe the faders as consistent and smooth (though not DJ-grade crossfader quality) and the pads as responsive. One Italian owner found the buttons soft and sensitive.
- **Improvements over the original.** One owner who used the first APC Mini says the MK2 feels heavier and sturdier, with a firmer USB connection, and that RGB pads made a huge difference.
- **Value against bigger controllers.** One owner calls it ideal for anyone who doesn't want to pay for an APC40, and another notes it costs about a quarter of a Launchpad X.

## Common complaints

**Pad brightness and colors (the most common hardware complaint)**
- Six owners found the pads dim or the colors dull. Several say they're hard to see in daylight or bright rooms, and one says the LEDs are fine in dark rooms. One owner who used the original model says orange looks pink and pink looks brown.
- Four owners couldn't get the pads to light up at all, or found the lighting stopped working. One owner says the RGB lights need Ableton Live 11.2.7 or newer.

**Setup, software and documentation**
- More than a dozen owners found setup confusing. Complaints include unclear instructions, a manual that doesn't explain much, a learning curve, and having to install several pieces of software, including the inMusic download manager and an iLok license.
- One owner explains that when Ableton isn't running, it shows up as two MIDI devices, and the second one is the Note mode output. They sometimes have to reset MIDI settings or open and close Ableton to get it working.
- Owners using it outside Ableton, for example with Voicemeeter, say there's very little documentation on the MIDI messages it uses. One owner who wrote their own MIDI tester says Akai support couldn't help.
- One owner found it hard to use alongside other controllers in Ableton, and another had volume jumps when switching songs while using it with an Ableton Push 2.
- One owner almost returned it because it didn't show up in their DAW. They say recent Windows MIDI updates don't yet work with some Akai products, and suggests searching online for a fix.

**Ableton Live Lite access**
- Several owners had trouble getting the included software. One owner's Ableton code had expired, another spent 3 days without getting Live Lite, and another couldn't download it on a Samsung laptop.
- One owner says only the Ableton key worked, not the keys for Akai's own software. Another was surprised it's not useful without Ableton, and one thought they needed a paid account.

**Reliability**
- Several owners say it worked at first and then failed: after a week, after 2 months, or with MIDI mappings that "went crazy" after a few months. One owner says there's no factory reset.
- Two owners had LEDs burn out. One has gone through three units, each losing its blue LED, the first after about a year and a half.
- One owner says it randomly stops working until you unplug it and plug it back in, which makes them wary of using it live.
- One owner's unit died after 5 minutes, and they felt it seemed lower quality than their previous Akai controllers. Others received units that wouldn't turn on, arrived damaged, or had a faulty USB port.

**Build, pads and faders**
- One owner says the build feels like a toy and the faders are just OK, and that it should be cheaper. Another owner says it's sturdy but shouldn't be treated roughly.
- One owner found the pads weak, and another found them a bit more sensitive than they like.
- One owner says Note mode isn't velocity sensitive.
- One owner wished it had USB-C instead of the older square USB-B port.

**Packaging**
- Three owners had shipping problems: a box with a large hole, a unit shipped in its retail box with no outer packaging, and a unit that arrived damaged.
