## Quick verdict

The **Novation Launchkey 49 MK4** is a 49-key DAW controller with semi-weighted keys, 16 pads with polyphonic aftertouch, 9 faders, pitch and mod wheels, an OLED screen, and built-in Arpeggiator, Chord and Scale modes. Owners love how tightly it works with Ableton Live, the faders and hands-on controls, and how quickly it's ready to play. The biggest complaints are the velocity response, keys that some find cheap or plasticky, and a frustrating start: no printed manual and a registration process some owners struggled with.

## Should you buy the Launchkey 49 MK4?

**Yes,** if you produce in Ableton Live (or another supported DAW) and want 49 keys plus faders, pads and transport controls so you can spend less time on the mouse. **No,** if you mainly want to play piano and care most about key feel and natural velocity, or if you want a controller that's easy to figure out without watching tutorials.

## What's in the box and the software

- Launchkey 49 MK4, USB cable and a safety information sheet (per the Amazon listing)
- **No printed manual.** Owners download the PDF manual from Novation's website.
- **Software comes by registration.** Owners say you register the unit with a code from a sticker on the back to get access codes for the included software. One owner mentions Ableton Live Intro and several good sound banks.

*The Amazon listing doesn't detail the software bundle. What's included may change.*

## What owners like

- **Ableton Live integration.** This is the most consistent praise. Owners call it very native to Ableton 12 and say it configures seamlessly. One owner uses the faders for last-second mixing and the play buttons instead of reaching for the laptop. Another especially likes the Capture MIDI button, which has its own button on the 49-key model.
- **Faders and hands-on control.** Owners call the 9 faders a big plus. One owner liked the buttons' feel more than an Ableton Push 2's, and found every control well printed and solid.
- **Key feel, for many.** Several owners describe the semi-weighted keys as smooth and firm but not too firm, and good enough for piano. One owner, who returned an Arturia KeyLab Essential MK3 because of plastic, clacky keys, found the Launchkey's build and keybed solid.
- **Quick setup for many.** One Logic Pro user found setup and the script integration a breeze and was playing within an hour. Others call it easy to use and straightforward.
- **Reason integration.** One owner who has used Reason for 15+ years calls the integration seamless.
- **Pads, for some.** One owner calls the pads very responsive, and another says they're the best-quality part of the controller.
- **Deep customization.** One owner notes that pads, buttons, faders, lights and even the screen can be customized through Novation's Programmer's Reference Guide, which makes it useful beyond DAWs, for example in Max/MSP.
- **Low power, light weight.** One owner says it draws about 0.5W, so it won't drain an iPad battery as fast, and finds it lighter than many other keyboards. The same owner carries it in a Roland CB-B37 keyboard bag and says it fits snugly.

## Common complaints

**Velocity response (the most common theme)**
- Five owners had trouble with the velocity response. They describe it as unnatural, too quiet even on the hard setting, or random, and one says you have to slam the keys to get any sound.
- One owner says that changing the velocity curve for normal playing made virtual instruments very quiet. Another had to turn velocity off to play piano properly.
- One owner found the black keys respond a little differently from the white keys and adjusted the settings to compensate.
- Not everyone agrees: one owner says the key sensors work flawlessly and allow very dynamic playing.

**Keys and build**
- Four owners found the keys or the build cheap-feeling, comparing it to budget keyboards. Two owners say the keys are slippery, and one found the black keys blocky.
- One owner found the semi-weighting barely noticeable.
- One owner says the faders are a bit soft, wobble sideways and have short travel, and that the top surface may scratch easily, but still found it robust overall.
- One owner says it's tall on a desk, at about 9 cm.

**Manual, registration and software access**
- Five owners say there was no manual in the box. Others found the manual hard to understand, with no simple "how to use the controller" guide, and one turned to YouTube videos.
- Two owners found registration and getting to the software frustrating. One called it the worst registration experience ever.
- One owner says some of the third-party software didn't work straight away.
- One owner found the controller hard to use without good English, since the videos and courses are in English.

**DAW issues**
- Two owners had Logic Pro problems. One says it didn't work with Logic 10.7.4, an older version, and the mixer controls didn't work well. Another found it incompatible with a Mac running Logic.
- One owner had delayed notes in every program on several PCs, and says Novation support blamed the computer.
- One owner wished it had buttons for browsing presets in Ableton, and kept going back to the mouse.

**Pads, encoders and creative modes**
- One owner found the pads noisy, small and unresponsive at the edges, with accidental triggers of neighboring pads. Another found them almost too sensitive to aftertouch.
- One owner says the encoders sometimes jump in value in Custom Mode when picking up a parameter.
- One owner says settings for the programmable wheel tend to disappear.
- Two owners felt the chord and arpeggiator features are easy to outgrow, and that plugins do the same job better.

**Units and shipping**
- One owner's "new" unit arrived used, with no manual or software. Another got no software codes and a USB cable that didn't fit. A third couldn't get any sound out of the unit at all.
- One owner disliked the USB-C port's position on the back.
- One owner says it draws a lot of power over USB, which contrasts with another owner's low-power report.
