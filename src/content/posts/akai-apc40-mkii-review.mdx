The **Akai Professional APC40 MKII** came out in 2014, and in 2026 it's still one of the most popular Ableton Live controllers on Amazon.

**Availability note:** Several retailers list the APC40 MKII as discontinued, though Akai hasn't announced it officially and it's still sold on Amazon. Stock may run low, so check the seller before you buy. If it's unavailable, see the alternatives below.

## Quick verdict

If you produce or perform in Ableton Live and want clip launching, mixing and device control in one compact box, the APC40 MKII is still the controller to beat. Owners of Akai's newer APC64 often say they miss it. The trade-off is durability: a minority of buyers report faders, buttons or knobs failing after months of heavy use. Buy it somewhere with an easy return policy and test every control early.

## Who is the APC40 MKII for?

**Best for** Ableton Live users who want hands-on mixing plus clip launching, especially for live sets.

**Skip it if** you use a different DAW, you mainly want to finger-drum, or you need a controller that can take serious road abuse.

## What do owners like about it?

- **It works the moment you plug it in.** Ableton recognizes the APC40 MKII with no setup. Buyers coming from controllers that needed manual configuration call this a big plus.
- **It feels solid.** A recent buyer who has owned plenty of cheaper gear says it doesn't have the hollow, soft-plastic feel of budget controllers.
- **Everything is on the surface at once.** This is the APC40's real strength, and it shows up most in reviews of other controllers. Owners who moved to the newer APC64 say the APC40 lets you launch clips, mix and tweak devices at the same time without switching modes. One APC64 buyer returned it and called the APC40 the best performance controller for Ableton, adding that nothing else on the market fills that gap yet.
- **Owners buy it twice.** Several long-time users wore out one unit and bought a second APC40 MKII instead of switching. They said no other controller, Novation's included, puts this many controls in one device.
- **Real knobs beat touch strips.** Another APC64 owner wrote that fine adjustments are hard with touch-sensitive faders, and said the APC40's physical knobs were much better for it.
- **Compact and portable.** At under 4 lb and about 17" wide, it fits on a desk next to a keyboard, or even on your lap. For carrying around every day, though, one owner takes the smaller APC Key 25 and leaves the APC40 at home.
- **Not just for music.** VJs pair it with Resolume Arena, one owner uses it to mix video live, and a lighting jockey runs their light shows from it.

## What are the common complaints?

- **Durability.** This is the main thing to know before you buy. The faders fail most often. One owner lost 3 faders on their first unit, and another wore theirs out mixing video. Others describe buttons launching the wrong clips, knobs coming loose, and a crossfader that drifts after about a year. Reports run from 2016 to 2021, so this isn't limited to early units. The good news is that fader pots can be replaced: one owner fixed an out-of-warranty unit by ordering new ones.
- **Slow repairs.** One gigging owner waited 2–4 weeks for a repair, then waited the same again for a replacement, and missed shows both times. If you perform live, keep a backup plan.
- **A risky button placement.** The Stop All Clips button sits right next to the master track button. At least one performer stopped an entire set by accident more than once before remapping it.
- **Custom mapping quirks.** Some users ran into limits when mapping pads to custom functions, such as a column of pads controlling the range of an effect.
- **Built for Ableton, mostly.** It works as a general MIDI controller on Mac and PC, but everything about it is designed around Ableton Live.

## How does it compare with the alternatives?

- **Akai APC64.** The newer model, with an 8×8 grid and touch strips instead of faders and knobs. Reviews are mixed. Several buyers describe the touch strips as hard to control precisely, and some returned it for the APC40.
- **Akai APC Mini MK2.** Much cheaper and smaller, with 64 pads and 9 faders but no knobs. A good choice if you only need clip launching and basic volume control. Reviews mention dimmer LEDs and more setup problems. See our [APC Mini MK2 review](/akai-apc-mini-mk2-review/).
- **Ableton Push.** Better as an instrument for finger drumming, playing melodies and sound design, but it doesn't give you the APC40's overview of the whole mixer.

## How should you buy one?

1. **Buy with a return window.** Amazon's return window gives you time to test the unit.
2. **Test every control in the first weeks.** Launch clips across the whole grid, sweep every knob and fader, and run the crossfader end to end.
3. **Consider extended protection if you'll gig with it.** The standard warranty is 1 year.
4. **Get a hard case.** Owners who tour with it use a hard-shell case to protect the faders.
5. **Remap Stop All if you perform live**, to avoid accidental stops.
