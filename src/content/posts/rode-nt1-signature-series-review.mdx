  - q: "Can I plug the NT1 Signature straight into my computer?"
    a: "No. It's an XLR mic that needs 48V phantom power, so you need an audio interface that supplies it. Several owners say they didn't realize this and had to buy an interface or extra cables separately."
  - q: "What comes in the box?"
    a: "The mic, an SM6 shock mount with pop filter, a 6m (20ft) XLR cable and a dust cover. There's no stand, boom arm, interface or USB cable. One owner wished it came with a case."
  - q: "How should I connect it?"
    a: "RØDE's user guide says to turn your interface's input volume fully down before connecting the XLR cable, to avoid feedback or loud sounds, then switch on 48V phantom power on your interface. One owner also suggests turning phantom power off before plugging it in, and another says they always switch phantom power off before connecting or disconnecting mics."
  - q: "Which side do I sing into?"
    a: "It's a side-address mic, so you sing into the side, not the top. RØDE says the gold dot on the body marks the front and should point toward your sound source."
  - q: "How do I put it together?"
    a: "The box has no printed diagram, according to one owner, but RØDE's online user guide covers it. Screw the pop filter into the socket on the L-shaped bracket of the SM6 mount. Then place the mic in the mount, turn it clockwise to screw it in loosely, point the gold dot at the pop filter, and tighten the ring under the mount while holding the mic."
  - q: "What's the blue ring in the box for?"
    a: "RØDE says it's for XLR cables that don't fit snugly in the mic's socket. You place it in the socket and push the cable in. The included cable doesn't need it."
  - q: "Does it have a pad or low-cut switch?"
    a: "No. One owner notes it has no low-cut (EQ) switch and no -10dB pad."
  - q: "Will it work in an untreated room?"
    a: "Owners disagree. One says it rejects noise from the sides and helps in a room that isn't soundproofed. Another says it sounds very bad without a suitable room. One owner with an air conditioner running uses a foam cover and the ReaFir plugin to make it usable."
  - q: "Can I use it on my desk?"
    a: "Owners recommend a boom arm. One says it's great on a boom arm but a poor mic sitting on a desk, and another recommends getting a boom arm. One owner uses it with a RØDE boom arm. RØDE says the SM6 mount fits any standard mic stand or studio arm, upright or upside down, with 5/8-inch and 3/8-inch threads."
  - q: "Does it work with a Focusrite Scarlett?"
    a: "Owners use it with Focusrite interfaces. One owner's mic cut in and out with a Scarlett Solo, but the replacement mic worked fine with the same interface."
  - q: "Is it good for recording instruments?"
    a: "RØDE says it suits vocals, guitars, drums, piano and more. One owner records spoken word and outdoor ambient sounds with it. Another, trying to record sounds for a sample kit, says it wouldn't pick up an acoustic guitar."
  - q: "What's the warranty?"
    a: "10 years with registration, per the Amazon listing. One owner whose mic stopped working says RØDE offered a warranty repair but they would have had to pay shipping."

## Quick verdict

The **RØDE NT1 Signature Series** is a large-diaphragm XLR condenser mic for vocals, voiceover, podcasting and streaming. It comes with a shock mount, pop filter and long XLR cable. Owners praise its clear, warm sound, low noise and the included accessories. The biggest complaint is reliability: many critical owners say it worked at first, then went silent, lost volume or started crackling, anywhere from minutes to a little over a year later. Other complaints are loose or raised XLR pins, and some owners hearing little difference from cheaper mics.

## Should you buy the NT1 Signature Series?

**Yes,** if you want your first XLR condenser for vocals or voiceover, already have (or will buy) an audio interface with phantom power, and can return it easily if your unit is faulty. **No,** if you want a USB mic that plugs straight into your computer, already own a decent condenser, or reliability is your top priority.

**A good fit if you:**
- Sing, record voiceovers or podcasts, or stream, and want a step up from a USB mic
- Want a shock mount, pop filter and cable in the box instead of buying them separately
- Will mount it on a boom arm or stand

**Look elsewhere if you:**
- Want to plug a mic straight into your computer. This one needs an audio interface with phantom power

- Need a -10dB pad or low-cut switch on the mic

## What's in the box

- NT1 Signature Series microphone
- SM6 shock mount with pop filter
- 6m (20ft) XLR cable
- Dust cover
- Blue ring for a snug fit with other XLR cables

*Contents are from the Amazon listing and RØDE's user guide. Not included: a stand or boom arm, an audio interface or a USB cable.*

## What owners like

- **Clear, warm sound.** Many owners describe their recordings as clear, warm, detailed or professional. One calls the default sound neutral and flat. Another says it sounds more expensive than it is.
- **Handles loud and soft singing.** One owner who records a classically trained singer says it captures both the softest and loudest passages clearly, without distortion. Another saw no peaking.
- **Low noise.** Owners mention very little mic noise and no hiss. One bought it specifically for its low noise floor.
- **Focuses on your voice.** One owner says the cardioid pattern rejects background noise from the sides, which helps in a room that isn't soundproofed. They also say plosives were almost absent with good mic technique.
- **Accessories in the box.** Owners like the shock mount, pop filter and long, good-quality XLR cable, and not having to buy them separately.
- **Solid feel.** Several owners say it feels solid, sturdy and well made. One finds it light without feeling cheap.
- **Value.** Several owners call it great value for the price.
- **Upgrade from a USB mic.** Owners coming from a desktop USB mic and a Blue Yeti say it was a big improvement.
- **Works with many setups.** Owners use it with Focusrite interfaces (see our [Scarlett Solo 3rd Gen review](/focusrite-scarlett-solo-3rd-gen-review/)), Reaper, GarageBand and Logic Pro, and for singing, voiceovers, calls, TikTok Live and outdoor recordings.

## Common complaints

**Units that stop working (the most common theme)**
- Ten owners in our sample say it worked at first, then stopped working or developed problems. The timing ranged from 10 minutes and one recorded song to a few days, a month, a few months, 8 months and a little over a year.
- They describe the mic going silent, the input level dropping sharply, crackling and level jumps with phantom power on, and new noise. One owner returned two units within a month. Another had two units fail, the first within a week and the replacement 4 days later.
- One owner says their mic failed after fewer than 15 hours of recording, despite storing it in a case. Another says they always turned phantom power off before connecting it.
- Three more owners say their mic didn't work at all, or picked up almost nothing, from the start.
- One owner's mic had inconsistent quality and gain that kept changing, even after switching to a second interface.

**XLR pins**
- Five owners found problems with the XLR connector: a pin sticking up above the others, a connector sticking out and loose, damaged prongs, and pins that started sliding in and out after the first time it was unplugged.

**Noise and volume**
- Some owners hear noise the mic shouldn't make: a constant odd noise, random hiss, and hiss when bumped or breathed on. One owner says it started adding noise after a month.
- Three owners found the signal very low. One tried different interfaces and cables, and another heard only a small difference with their interface gain at 100%.

**Sound vs. cheaper mics**
- Several owners hear little difference from cheaper or similar mics, including the MXL 990, Blue Bluebird, AT2020, Blue Yeti and HyperX QuadCast. One says without the right gear it doesn't sound much different from a $100 mic.
- One owner thinks the AKG P420 sounds better and was a better buy. Another says their Yeti sounds better.
- One owner who also has the NT1 4th Gen says the Signature Series has much cheaper build quality.

**Setup and assembly**
- One owner says there are no assembly instructions in the box, so they had to look up how to put the mic and pop filter together. RØDE's online user guide covers this (see the FAQ). Another found it difficult to put together, and one found the shock mount hard to screw in the first time.
- Two owners had to buy an audio interface or a cable to connect to their computer separately.
- One owner says it's not beginner friendly.

**Condition on arrival**
- Owners report a scratched white mic, a damaged package, a missing cable, and a missing blue ring and sticker that made one owner wonder if the unit was a resold return.
- One owner blames a third-party Amazon seller for a faulty unit and says other reviews mention the same seller.

**Support**
- One owner whose mic stopped passing signal says RØDE first asked them to store it away from humidity, then offered a warranty repair if they paid shipping.

**Other**
- One owner says it's a poor desk mic without a boom arm.
- One owner says it wouldn't pick up an acoustic guitar.
- One owner found the cable too long.
